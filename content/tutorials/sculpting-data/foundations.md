Data comes from outside: a recording, a picture, a 3D model, a film, a microphone, a camera. MayaFlux does not care where it came from. It can be played or shown as it is, or taken apart, measured and rebuilt into something its maker never heard or saw.

This part starts with the data as it arrives, and works toward what you can make of it:

- Containers hold large collections of data, and buffers use one step of them
- A domain says where data goes: quickly with `|`, or by hand when the choice matters
- A recording can be read from at any speed, in any direction, by any number of voices
- A recording can be cut into grains, measured, and put back in an order you choose
- A picture is data a shader reads, and a sound can steer where it reads
- Many pictures, stills, films and moments of the past, can be held as one stack, and a shader decides what the pile becomes
- A shape is a list of points and triangles, and a sound can be its outline, its surface or its path

Each section begins with a snippet to run first. The explanations under it stay closed until you open them, and every section ends with **Try It → Recap**.

How to work through it:

- Pick a domain: sound, picture, film, a model, a table
- Run card 1 to bring that data in
- Jump to the card that works on it and run its first snippet
- Tweak the numbers, one at a time, and run again
- If you can follow what a change did, open the explanations to learn what is happening underneath, and change it again with more reach
- Then pick another domain and repeat

*Form is data. It can come from anywhere, and it can go anywhere.*
